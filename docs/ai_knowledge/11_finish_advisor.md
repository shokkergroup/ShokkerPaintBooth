## Finish advice: what the built-in advisor does (and what to do when it cannot)
Ask in plain words and the Shokker copilot answers WITHOUT an AI key: "what finish should I put on the stripes to make them pop", "show me chrome finishes", "candy vs pearl", "what finish is on my hood right now", "which finishes have the most sparkle", "something that looks like brushed aluminum". It answers with 3-6 REAL catalogue finishes as swatch cards (shown on your own car colour): each card has a one-line reason, what kind of finish it is, a **Use on the stripes / hood / body** button (one click applies it, with Undo) and a **Details** button. Follow-ups work: "show me more", "something calmer", "something bolder", "what about satin instead", "and the roof?". Orders such as "make my stripes pop" apply the first pick (Undo takes it back) and keep the others as cards. Yes/no questions ("will chrome numbers be readable") get a verdict, "review my finishes" lists what to change on the current scheme, and with a picture attached **What finish is this?** (AI) names the closest catalogue finishes. It never changes the car until you press Use. Plain design orders ("make the stripes chrome") still go to the designer and are applied straight away. If the advisor cannot find anything it falls back to the AI (when a key is set) or the manual.

## Finish advice: which finishes keep my colours (shine only) and which repaint
Each card says what the finish does to colour. **"shine only: keeps your paint colours"** = a Foundation finish (Gloss, Satin, Matte, Pearl, Metallic, Candy, Chrome, Satin Chrome, Brushed ...): it changes only HOW the surface shines (the spec map) and your existing paint colours stay. **"takes the zone colour"** = the finish needs a colour from the zone (set the zone colour; Candy over red gives candy red). **"brings its own colours"** = a complete special look (galaxy, oil slick, holographic ...) with its own palette: it replaces the colour. For an existing livery with sponsors, start with the "keeps your paint colours" ones.

## Finish advice: how to choose for stripes, numbers, hood and body
- Thin accents (stripes, lower band, trim): they have to read at a glance. A DIFFERENT SHINE from the body does it (matte stripes on a gloss body, or gloss stripes on a flat body); a bright metal edge (satin chrome) or candy glow also works. Chrome is great on thin lines and loud on big areas.
- Numbers: clean edges and no glare. Gloss, satin or matte; the colour contrast behind the digits matters more than the finish. Avoid chrome, glitter and colour-shifting finishes on numbers (they break up the edges at speed).
- Big areas (hood, roof, body): pearl, candy, metallic, satin or gloss in the livery colour read premium; one real material (carbon, brushed metal) on one panel lifts the whole car.
- Adjacent areas must differ in colour or in shine; 2-3 colours plus one accent looks expensive.
- Premium = restraint. Shimmer looks expensive when it is fine (pearl, fine metallic) and in one place; real glitter is best as one accent.

## Finish glossary: gloss, satin, matte, pearl, metallic, candy, chrome (plain words)
- Gloss: standard shiny paint, sharp reflections; the safe default under numbers and sponsors. Satin: low sheen between gloss and matte, hides small flaws. Matte: no clear-coat shine, soft flat colour; the stealth look.
- Pearl: tiny pearlescent particles, soft shimmer and a slight colour shift with the angle. Metallic: fine aluminium flake, sparkle in sun. Candy: translucent, strongly tinted coat over bright metal, so the colour looks very deep.
- Chrome: mirror metal that reflects the surroundings; Satin Chrome: the same metal with soft reflections; Dark Chrome: chrome with a black cast. Brushed: fine grain like raw metal. Carbon fibre: woven pattern under clear coat.
- In the sim iRacing multiplies the paint colour by the metal level in the spec, so any normal colour turned fully metallic goes dark (a chrome part has to be painted white or nearly white): partial-metal finishes (metallic, pearl, satin chrome) keep their colour better. Finish never changes how the car drives, only how it looks.

## Finish search: how the whole catalogue is understood (finish cards, three lanes, more options)
Every one of the ~4,800 catalogue items (finishes, paint patterns, spec patterns) has a CARD written once by an AI from its picture and its measurements: a plain-words look, what it resembles, 10-16 words a painter might type, mood, era, car class, best placement, loudness 1-5, busyness 1-5, what it pairs with and what to avoid. Search uses the cards, so "dusty dirt late model", "whiskey amber candy", "something expensive like a watch face" or "fighter jet grey" find real matches instead of words in a name. Suggestions come in THREE lanes: **keep** (shine-only finishes that keep your paint colours), **complete** (complete looks that bring their own palette; they replace the colour, so they are ranked by how well the palette suits YOUR colours), **texture** (a spec pattern that changes only the shine, or a paint pattern, layered on top). Say "show me more" for options that never repeat, "bolder" / "calmer" to move the dial, or describe the mood, car class or era you want. Thin accents prefer plain finishes with a different shine from the body; numbers get plain finishes only.

## Finish kits: a finish plan for the whole car in one step
Ask "suggest a finish combo for the whole car" (or "what finishes go together", "finish package for everything") and the copilot proposes THREE kits: **Classic** (proven, calm, readable), **Premium** (pearl, metal, candy depth) and **Bold** (stronger finishes plus a complete-look hero panel on the hood). **Premium** and **Bold** also add a fine shine texture (a spec pattern: it changes only the shine pattern, your colours stay) on the hood or roof. A kit covers the body, the stripes and the numbers that your car actually has; the roles are chosen TOGETHER: the accents get a different shine from the body (that is what makes thin lines read), the numbers always stay plain and readable, and no kit repeats another kit's finishes. **Use this kit** applies it in one step (one Undo). "Show me different kits" gives three new ones.

## Talking it through: follow-ups that point at what you were just shown
The copilot remembers the list it just showed, so you can talk to it like a person:
- **Point at one:** "tell me about the second one", "I like the second one but more sparkly", "use the third one".
- **Like X but ...:** "something like Undertow but calmer", "like chrome but darker", "texture like carbon fiber but finer". Changes it understands: calmer, bolder, darker, lighter, glossier, flatter, more / less sparkle, more / less metallic, warmer, cooler, more colourful, more muted, simpler, busier, finer, coarser, and a colour ("... but in blue"). Say "more like this" to page through more of the same.
- **Bare tweaks:** after any list just say "darker", "less metallic", "a bit shinier": it re-finds finishes close to what you were looking at, moved that way.
- **Why:** "why did you pick that", "why not chrome": it explains from the finish's card (look, loudness / busyness, how it is rated for that part, cautions) or says honestly when there is no real reason against it.
- **What goes with:** "what goes with chrome" gives accents that read next to it (a different shine); "what finish goes with candy red" gives finishes for that colour.
- **Which is best:** "which of these is best for night racing" re-ranks the list you just saw for that goal and names a pick.
- **Say what you do NOT want:** "no glitter", "nothing holographic or chameleon", "I hate chrome", "don't make it look like a wrap": everything matching is left out of EVERY later answer in the conversation (anything with glitter, chrome, holographic, wrap looks ...). "Not too shiny" or "not just purple sparkle" are degrees, not exclusions.
- **Textures:** "what texture would look good on the stripes", "add a subtle pattern to the body", "what spec patterns work on matte" open the texture lane (spec patterns change only the shine; paint patterns add colour); "show me more" and "calmer" stay in that lane.
- **Two parts in one sentence:** "I want the numbers plain but the stripes to really pop", "keep the hood subtle but give the roof something wild", "what should I put on the hood and what on the roof": it answers each part separately ("Two parts, two answers"), each card says which part it is for, and **Use** applies it to that part only.
