# SPB Build Logs — Narrative Iterations

> Real painters iterate. These logs show the *mess* behind a finished livery — the wrong turns, the backtracks, the "I tried X because Y, switched to Z." Read for the mental model, not the final answer.

Each log is presented version-by-version with commentary.

---

## Build Log #1 — "Shop Street Rod"

**Brief:** Personal build. A mid-60s-flavored street rod for a show next weekend. Goal: period credibility with a modern twist. Budget of time: one weekend.

### v1 — Friday night, 9 PM

- Body: `gloss_wrap` candy red
- Trim: chrome everywhere
- Graphics: none

**Painter note:** "Just wanted to see a base. Red gloss + chrome is the starting vocabulary for a street rod. First render looked like a rental from a costume party. Too clean."

### v2 — Friday night, 10:30 PM

- Swapped base to `candy_apple` (deeper, richer)
- Kept chrome
- Added thin gold pinstripe along waistline

**Painter note:** "Candy_apple is a jewel, not a paint. Instantly read richer. Gold pinstripe at the belt gave the car an anchor. But the roof was still naked and the car looked like a cartoon without a hat."

### v3 — Saturday morning, 9 AM (fresh eyes)

- Base stays `candy_apple`
- Roof swapped to `pearl_base` cream
- Pinstripe extended to match roof edge

**Painter note:** "Cream roof dropped the temperature of the car. Now it reads period, not cartoon. But pinstripe feels too thin — 1mm in the render is basically invisible."

### v4 — Saturday, 10 AM

- Pinstripe bumped from 1mm to 3mm
- Added a second 1mm pinstripe below the 3mm (double-stripe motif)

**Painter note:** "Double pinstripe is a 50s cue. Now it reads kustom shop without trying. Something still wrong though. Car's too clean — looks like it was painted yesterday."

### v5 — Saturday, 11 AM (tried damage overlay)

- Added `damage_wear_v2` at 30%

**Painter note:** "WAY too much. Car looked like it survived a hurricane. A street rod in 1965 was kept *clean* — not aged. Rolled back the damage layer entirely. Wrong direction."

### v6 — Saturday, 12:30 PM (lunch break, came back with idea)

- Added slight `pearl_base` warm undertone to the candy_apple for subtle richness
- Tightened the pinstripe gold to a warmer, more buttery shade (not yellow-gold, rich-gold)

**Painter note:** "Those two tweaks did more than most of the previous iterations. Candy now has a *quality* to it. Gold is buttery, not brassy. Feels like a restoration."

### v7 — Saturday, 3 PM (after a walk)

- Wheels upgraded: chrome wires with candy red center caps
- Calipers: matching red

**Painter note:** "Wheels were the footnote I never finished. Chrome wires = 60s royalty. Now the whole car is coherent."

### v8 — Saturday, 6 PM

- Added a small pinstripe monogram on front quarter — not a sponsor, just a shop logo
- Tiny, maybe 2 inches tall, gold

**Painter note:** "Monogram is the equivalent of a painter signing the canvas. Felt right. But I noticed the front and rear of the car felt unbalanced. Rear felt naked."

### v9 — Sunday, 10 AM

- Added matching monogram on rear quarter opposite side
- Tiny gold script on trunk lid — "since '64" in period script

**Painter note:** "Now it has narrative. This is a car *from* somewhere. Period script was important — modern font would have killed it."

### v10 — Sunday, 3 PM (final pass)

- Reviewed every decision
- Removed one thing — a second small pinstripe that was "cool but unnecessary"

**Painter note:** "Final submission. Looked at v10 vs v1 and the car is unrecognizable. Key lesson: the iterations weren't cumulative — they were conversations. Some versions went backwards (v5) and that's fine. That's how you figure out where the line is."

**Final time invested:** 14 hours across two days
**Final decisions that mattered most:** Pearl roof (v3), pinstripe thickness (v4), walk-away-and-come-back (v6), wheel coordination (v7), monogram narrative (v9).
**Decisions that didn't matter:** Trying 5 different candy tints, messing with 3 different chrome finishes.

## Build Log #2 — "League Race Week"

**Brief:** A sim-league season car. Sponsors locked, team colors locked. Five iterations max — race is Saturday. Commercial discipline required.

### v1 — Monday evening

- Team colors: red + white
- Body split: red upper, white lower, hard edge at beltline
- Primary sponsor: hood
- Number 47: door roundel
- Car number also on roof

**Painter note:** "Bare minimum template. Zero risk. Boring but readable. Know I'll tweak from here."

### v2 — Tuesday

- Changed split to diagonal (rear-low to front-high)
- Added 3mm white pinstripe along the diagonal

**Painter note:** "Diagonal adds motion. Pinstripe cleans up where the two colors meet. But the diagonal makes the car look unbalanced — the front looks heavy, the back looks light."

### v3 — Wednesday

- Rebalanced diagonal — flipped direction, now front-low to rear-high
- Added secondary sponsor on rear quarter (smaller)
- Number size audit: door number too big for TV, reduced 15%

**Painter note:** "Flipped diagonal feels better — implies forward motion. Sponsor audit was overdue. Still something missing — front fascia feels naked, and that's where TV focuses during starts."

### v4 — Thursday (crunch time)

- Added small front-fascia graphic: 3-inch team logo centered on nose
- Also added a very thin 1mm red pinstripe along the entire beltline
- Chrome trim added only to front grille (subtle)

**Painter note:** "Front fascia fixed. Chrome grille was a risk — it's a modern team, not a classic one — but test-render showed it adds gravitas without feeling costume. Keeping it."

### v5 — Friday (submission morning)

- Final audit
- Removed the 1mm red pinstripe at beltline — it was too subtle and added nothing
- Slightly desaturated the red body color by 5% — TV broadcast test render was too hot

**Painter note:** "Subtraction on the last day. Shipping. Lesson: deadlines force ruthless editing. No time for vanity details."

**Final time invested:** 8 hours spread across four evenings
**Decisions that mattered most:** Diagonal direction (v3), TV-calibrated number size (v3), front-fascia logo (v4), desaturation for broadcast (v5).
**Decisions that didn't matter:** First diagonal direction, extra pinstripe, wavering on chrome.

## Build Log #3 — "Restoration Research"

**Brief:** Recreate a specific 1973 privateer endurance car from three period photographs. Historical accuracy is non-negotiable.

### Phase 1 — Monday through Wednesday (research, no paint)

- Gathered the three photographs
- Identified the color (period burgundy — warmer and slightly browner than modern burgundy)
- Identified the font (period German sans-serif — very specific)
- Identified stripe width (4.5 inches based on door-handle proportions)
- Identified sponsor placements and period-correct decal sizes
- Noted the car had patina — rock chips on front fascia, dust on lower skirt

**Painter note:** "Three days of reading, zero painting. This is the 80% that beginners skip and that separates a costume from a recreation."

### v1 — Thursday morning

- Base: `candy_burgundy` (closest catalog match)
- Twin stripes: 4.5 inches, navy + cream
- Period font for all text
- No patina yet (wanted clean base first)

**Painter note:** "First render. Car reads as 70s but too clean. Still, I don't want to add patina until I confirm the base is right."

### v2 — Thursday afternoon

- Tweaked the burgundy — slightly desaturated, slightly warmer (period burgundies were dulled by time and period paint chemistry)
- Confirmed against photograph 1 — close match now

**Painter note:** "Spent 40 minutes color-matching. Every period-correct build lives or dies on color calibration. Modern candy_burgundy is beautiful but too vivid for 1973. The fix was a 6% desaturation + 3° warm shift."

### v3 — Thursday evening

- Added sponsor decals with period-correct fonts and sizes
- Verified placement against photographs 2 and 3
- Photograph 3 revealed a small decal I hadn't noticed — a scrutineer's sticker on the windscreen. Added it.

**Painter note:** "Third photograph always reveals the thing you missed. This is why you work from multiple reference shots, not just one."

### v4 — Friday morning

- Added patina
- Rock chips on front fascia: 12% intensity, concentrated on leading edge
- Dust on lower skirt: 8% warm tan overlay
- Tape residue near stripes: very subtle, barely visible

**Painter note:** "This is the step most painters skip. A period car is NEVER showroom-clean. The patina IS the signature of use."

### v5 — Friday afternoon (cross-check)

- Showed renders to a period-sim-racing friend
- He caught that the number font weight was slightly too heavy — period originals used a lighter weight

**Painter note:** "Never submit historical work without peer review. Even one expert eye catches what you miss. Fixed the font weight, re-rendered."

### v6 — Saturday morning (final)

- Final audit against all three reference photographs
- Everything matches within visual tolerance
- Stripe alignment has a tiny intentional off-axis tilt — matches photograph 2 where the tape laid slightly imperfect

**Painter note:** "That tiny tilt matters. Modern precision would have betrayed the era. The imperfection IS the quality."

**Final time invested:** 3 days of research + 12 hours of paint = realistically a week
**Key lesson:** For historical work, research dominates. Painting is the last 20% of effort.

## Build Log #4 — "Weekend Experiment"

**Brief:** No deadline, no sponsor, no constraint. Pure exploration. Goal: find something new.

### v1 — Saturday, 11 AM

- Started with `chameleon_base` because I've been curious but haven't committed to one
- Went red-orange-gold shift
- Saw immediate "volcano" vibe — leaned in

**Painter note:** "Pure play. Sometimes you start with a finish, not a concept."

### v2 — Saturday, 12 PM

- Added gloss black hood
- Gloss black rear diffuser
- Thought: black anchors the shift, prevents it from floating

**Painter note:** "Volcano gets a bed-rock. Now the shift reads as the *heat* of the car, not just the paint."

### v3 — Saturday, 2 PM (after lunch)

- Tried adding chrome trim
- Looked wrong immediately — chrome reflects the environment, which disrupts the chameleon story

**Painter note:** "Scratched it. Went back to v2. This is an important lesson — some finishes fight each other. Chameleon wants to be seen; chrome wants to show something else."

### v4 — Saturday, 4 PM

- Replaced chrome trim attempt with gloss black trim
- Confirmed: chameleon wants dark anchors, not bright ones

**Painter note:** "Black trim is invisible on a black hood, so the car now has basically no trim. This is aggressive minimalism and I love it."

### v5 — Saturday, 6 PM

- Added a single 3mm gold pinstripe at beltline
- Gold echoed the warmest band of the chameleon shift

**Painter note:** "One gold line. That's it. The car has a heartbeat now — one human line on a living surface. This is the whole design."

### v6 — Sunday, 11 AM

- Came back, fresh eyes
- Debated removing the gold pinstripe (Nina's voice in my head: "remove one more thing")
- Tried it
- Car felt less complete

**Painter note:** "Confirmed the gold pinstripe belongs. Important: just because a minimalist would remove it doesn't mean I should. Test the removal, then decide."

### v7 — Sunday, 2 PM

- Added wheel finish: gloss black with subtle chameleon accent on the spokes
- Calipers: gold, matching the pinstripe

**Painter note:** "Wheels tied everything together. Chameleon spokes make the wheels part of the car, not an afterthought. Calipers + pinstripe = color rhyme."

### v8 — Sunday, 4 PM (final)

- Sat with it for an hour
- Made zero changes
- Submitted

**Painter note:** "Sometimes you know when it's done. No anxious tweaks, no last-minute second-guessing. If you've done the iterations honestly, the final version *feels* final."

**Final time invested:** 8 hours, mostly on Saturday
**Key lesson:** Exploration builds have no brief but aren't directionless — you let the finish and the composition tell you where to go. You listen.

## Cross-Cutting Lessons

1. **Every build has dead ends.** v5 in Log #1 was a full wrong direction. Normal. Even expected.
2. **Walk away at least once per day.** Every log has a "came back fresh" moment.
3. **Iterate small after v5.** Early iterations are big moves. Late iterations are 2mm adjustments.
4. **The boring version has to exist first.** Log #2 v1 was intentionally plain. You can only edit what's already there.
5. **Research replaces iteration.** Log #3 had less painting because the research was deeper.
6. **Submit when the tweaks stop improving.** Log #4 v8 made zero changes and shipped. That's the signal.
7. **Photographs reveal lies.** Every log has at least one "the screenshot caught a problem my eyes missed" moment.
8. **Removing is as creative as adding.** More than one version above ends in a deletion.
