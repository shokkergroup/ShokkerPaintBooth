## 1. apply_wear makes matte glossy — FIXED

- **Evidence (before):** any wear > 0 forced B to exactly 16 everywhere (max gloss).
  matte B_mean 153 -> 16.0 at wear 50 and 100; satin 67 -> 16.0; direct
  `apply_wear` on a flat B=200 spec -> min = max = 16. Lit spheres show a matte car
  growing a sharp gloss hot-spot at wear 50.
- **Root cause:** `shokker_engine_v2.py` `apply_wear` clearcoat block did
  `np.clip(current_cc - cc_damage*16, 0, 16)` — subtracting (glossier) AND capping
  at 16; the edge-wear step also subtracted (`- edge_wear*10`).
- **Fix:** `shokker_engine_v2.py:22989` (audit comment) — `B += cc_damage*140`,
  clip [16,255]; edge wear `B += edge_wear*60`. Roughness / metallic / chips /
  edge roughness untouched. Callers (per-zone batched wear ~20450; season/global
  wear ~23350 car/helmet/suit) need no change.
- **After:** matte 153 -> 176 (w50) -> 198 (w100); satin 67 -> 90 -> 114;
  gloss 16 -> 39 -> 63 (p95 136), patchy where the damage noise is high.
- **Images:** `bugfix_2026-10-04/wear_before.png` vs `wear_after.png`
  (rows matte/satin/gloss; cols wear 0/50/100, each a B crop + lit sphere).
- **What I saw:** before, the B crops at wear 50/100 are solid black (16) and the
  matte sphere gains a pin-sharp highlight; after, the B crops show cloudy brighter
  (duller) patches, matte stays soft, gloss gets mottled dull patches. Reads as a
  weathered coat; the +140 strength is my pick — owner eye should confirm.
