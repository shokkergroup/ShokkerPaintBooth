## COLOR SHOXX hypothesis

Use separate, strongly contrasting pigment populations attached to different roughness/metallic/coat regions. Owner clarified red/blue was only an example: each of ten gets its own pairing (scarlet/cobalt, orange/cyan, magenta/lime, violet/yellow, scarlet/ice, emerald/orange, royal blue/pink, lemon/blue, lilac/chartreuse, turquoise/vermilion). Broad diffuse response should favour one population; a concentrated reflection should favour the other. Ten unique constructions explore different occupancy, lobe widths and which pigment owns the conductor. A numerical microfacet daylight sweep is an engineering diagnostic, not an iRacing shader reproduction or proof of a successful on-track flip. Coarse mip averaging can destroy the separation; show that limitation in evidence.

Sources: [PBRT microfacet roughness](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Reflection_Models/Roughness_Using_Microfacet_Theory), [PBRT conductor reflection](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Reflection_Models/Conductor_BRDF), [iRacing clearcoat introduction](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000168823-2023-season-1-release-notes-2022-12-06-01-). The design is an inference from these mechanisms, not a claim that a stock spec map supplies angle-dependent pigment, normals or interference.

## MAD SCIENTIST color requirement

### COLOR SHOXX: what the evidence does and does not show

The ten pigment pairings are varied deliberately; red/blue is only Janus Blades. A simplified GGX daylight sweep measures chromaticity movement, but its strongest glints frequently wash toward white. Downsampling to 128px weakens the apparent separation substantially. This is **not proof of a jarring daytime iRacing flip**, and the viewer does not render a fake flip animation. The work delivered here is ten distinct, exportable attempts at competing pigment/material populations.

The next useful assessment is on the same curved car panel in actual daylight, with exposure, weather, time and camera held fixed while the car changes heading. Compare ordinary race camera distance as well as a close view: mip averaging may erase an effect visible only close up. Do not promote a white-highlight washout as a two-color flip. No angle-dependent pigment, emission, normal map, diffraction or new physical mathematics is claimed.
