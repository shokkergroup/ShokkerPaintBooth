# FORGE — make the next livery faster without losing its design

Owner correction, September 11, 2026: “I think you may have massaged these enough.” Current paint polishing is parked. This is a workflow change for future work, not a claim that every current paint is final or every UV surface is calibrated.

## What actually helped

- **Save template knowledge once.** The official 2026 DLM Wire matched the existing profile. The missing deck/roof margins came from using camera-visible patches as full islands. Reuse the official baseline, full physical artwork masks, protected parts and measured placement maps as separate resources. Audit the whole template before building a batch; fix a shared coverage mistake once rather than rediscovering it car by car.
- **Build the artwork before projecting it.** Detailed, integrated background fields retained the concept much better than rebuilding it from a few isolated logos and motifs. Inventory its defining elements once: composition, illustration, texture, palette, lettering and number style. Use existing good sheets; generate only missing coverage. Ten sheets are a requested pack format, not ten mandatory new generations when equivalent assets already exist.
- **Keep a real editable scene.** Complete background art goes beneath separate numbers and sponsors. Preserve source assets and transforms. Produce the tidy eight-layer master, requested detailed master and simple three-layer version from the same scene. Never erase number/logo silhouettes from the backing.
- **Use Studio to review paint quickly.** Chrome SHOKKER DLM Studio is useful for composition and obvious placement defects. Its surface creases can distort otherwise sound paint: the Chess solid-color/no-spec probe demonstrated this. Do not warp real-template artwork to cancel a prototype defect. Use iRacing when exact seam/direction evidence is needed and available; report the distinction once.

## The next-job workflow

1. **Load a small resume packet:** current delivery/scene, chosen concept, template fingerprint, coverage mask manifest and unresolved owner-visible defects. Reuse working dependencies and exporters. Do not read the historical archive or reinstall tooling by default.
2. **Lock the design and sources:** retain the approved composition; use actual sponsor/SPB assets. Make high-resolution master art once, then sample for the required native template size. Upscaling may improve a useful source but does not recover missing design or fix bad text.
3. **Assemble once with the saved template:** fill complete islands and returns, including art hidden by decals. Declare intentional solid areas. Keep one background owner per shared hood/front surface. Do not stretch, mirror or repeat unique figures to make an alpha test pass.
4. **Review one compact view set:** flat official wire with decals hidden, overhead, both sides and a front/rear angle as the design needs. Compare at useful car-view size before inspecting tiny pixels. Group corrections by root cause: artwork, placement/coverage, material or viewer geometry.
5. **Fix the short defect list in one focused pass.** A second pass is for a demonstrable remaining problem. More passes need a named visible defect and a different corrective hypothesis, not a wish to keep improving. This is a planning default, not permission to leave a broken export or ignore a specific owner correction.
6. **Export once after visual selection:** TGA/spec plus requested PSD variants. Run file/source/backing checks for the chosen candidate. Re-render only affected views after a change; reuse unchanged evidence when its paint/spec and relevant settings match. Capture the requested 4K presentation set at delivery, rather than every experiment. Freeze the candidate when the requirements are met and remaining differences are immaterial to the design; do not claim owner approval without it.

## Stop repeating these expensive mistakes

| Previous waste | Replacement |
|---|---|
| Rebuilding each car to discover the same missing island | Shared full-template coverage checklist and versioned masks; qualify one example before propagation |
| Endless new versions, PSD packs and all-angle proofs | Working candidate for iteration; publish only a visibly improved selected revision |
| Metrics becoming the objective | Coverage proves listed masks only; valid PSD proves file properties only; likeness still needs comparison |
| Repeated artifact verification with no changes | Cache evidence by relevant inputs; rerun when the candidate, exporter or concern changes |
| Regenerating clean logos with AI, then repairing lettering | Reuse quality originals; keep text and identity independently editable |
| Reopening completed regions during unrelated fixes | Freeze accepted regions; change only the layers needed for the named defect |
| Repeated “still working” updates and giant handoffs | Brief outcome updates; one current state, one defect list, one next action |

## DLM defaults retained

Roof numbers face forward or the passenger side, never default to driver-facing. Deck text generally faces the spoiler unless the design calls for another orientation. Use actual SHOKKER PAINT BOOTH identity and “Two in the Paint, One in the Spec”; leave readable insets, particularly inside the spoiler. Optional DECAL LOGOS need not stay on. Preserve mandatory template details and exclude Wire/mask guides from paint exports. These rules do not identify an unknown UV island by themselves.

## Honest stopping and learning

When the owner says enough, park cosmetic polish. Keep unresolved material defects recorded without silently turning them into an automatic repaint queue. Do not request another approval merely to obey that instruction. A future continuation starts from the current scope, not an old unchecked backlog.

Retain only reusable lessons: observation → cause/evidence → working correction → scope → limitation. Template facts belong in the profile; design offsets belong beside the car; temporary process handles belong in the live job record. These are saved instructions and assets, not model-weight training.

For the next new car, record elapsed time by asset preparation, assembly, review and export, plus rejected candidates and repeated work. Compare to the next job before claiming a speedup; no measured speedup is claimed by this document.
