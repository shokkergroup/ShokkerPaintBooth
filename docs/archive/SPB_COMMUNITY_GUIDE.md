# Shokker Paint Booth — Community Guide

SPB is more than a tool — it's a community of painters, leagues, and sim-racers who share work, give feedback, and push each other to paint better. This guide covers how to find the community, how to share your own work, how to give and receive feedback, and how to participate without burning bridges.

---

## Where to Find the Community

The SPB community lives in several places. Join the ones that match your style.

### Discord — the fastest back-and-forth

The SPB Discord is the heartbeat of the community. You'll find:
- **#showcase** — post finished liveries for the community to react to.
- **#work-in-progress** — share 80%-done work for live feedback.
- **#inspiration** — share references, concept art, other people's liveries that inspire you.
- **#leagues** — list your league, recruit drivers, share configs.
- **#help** — ask technical questions about SPB features.
- **#tutorials** — members share how-to threads on specific techniques.
- **#sponsor-packs** — contribute or download shared decal packs.
- **#color-clinic** — post a palette for feedback before painting.

Invite link: available in the SPB main menu under Community → Join Discord.

### Forums — for deeper threads

The SPB forums host longer-form content — full tutorials, theory debates, seasonal retrospectives. Threads stay searchable for years, unlike Discord messages that scroll away.

### Reddit (r/sim_racing_paints)

A broader sim-racing painting community. SPB users cross-post here. Good for feedback from painters using other tools too.

### YouTube and Twitch

A handful of SPB creators stream painting sessions. Watching a pro paint in real time is one of the fastest ways to level up. Check the community wiki for active creators.

### Conventions and in-person

Some regions host sim-racing meetups where painters gather. Check the Events section of the forums.

## Sharing Your Paint

Before you share, consider:

**Render before you post.** SPB's final render is always higher quality than the real-time preview. Export a final render before posting. Include the 3D car rotation GIF if you can — movement helps sell a livery.

**Give your post context.** A good post includes:
- The car and series the livery is for.
- Any league or event it was built for.
- The color palette you picked.
- Which finish choices matter (candy? pearl? matte?).
- What you're proud of; what you'd change.

**Use multiple angles.** At minimum: front three-quarter, side profile, rear three-quarter. Bonus: top-down, close-up on the hood graphic, close-up on the door number.

**Credit inspiration.** If your livery is inspired by a heritage archetype, say so. The community respects painters who name their sources. Plagiarism of other community members' work is frowned upon; inspiration and homage are welcome when acknowledged.

**Don't post low-effort.** A single preview screenshot with no description gets ignored. A well-framed post gets feedback and reactions.

## Getting Feedback

Feedback is the fastest way to grow. Asking well matters.

**Ask specifically.** "What do you think?" gets vague responses. "Does the centerline stripe feel too wide? Should the number be bigger?" gets useful responses.

**Post at the right stage.** Post when you're 70-80% done — you still have energy and openness to change things. Post at 100% and feedback becomes "I wish you'd done X" regret.

**Take feedback gracefully.** Not every critique will be right. Not every critique will be delivered kindly. Filter signal from noise. Thank people either way.

**Return the favor.** If you got helpful feedback on your work, give feedback on someone else's within the week. The community runs on reciprocity.

**Don't defend.** The moment you explain why your livery is good, you stop learning. Let the feedback land. You can disagree quietly and keep your design.

## Contributing Back

The community runs because members contribute. Ways to contribute:

### Recipe contributions

Wrote a step-by-step for building a specific livery style? Post it to #tutorials. Even a short Discord thread helps new painters.

### Sponsor pack contributions

Designed a clean sponsor decal? Export it as a `.shokker` snippet and upload to #sponsor-packs. Name it clearly. Other painters can drop it into their work.

### Inspiration reference contributions

Found a reference image (non-copyrighted, license-clear) that could inspire others? Post to #inspiration. Link the source.

### Bug reports and feature requests

Found an SPB bug? Report on GitHub with reproduction steps. Thought of a feature? Post to #feature-requests with a clear user story.

### Beta testing

Major SPB releases cycle through beta testing. Volunteer via the forum sticky. You get early access; the team gets real-world testing.

### Teaching

Host a paint-along stream. Write a beginner's walkthrough. Answer questions in #help. Nothing grows the community like experienced painters helping new ones.

### Community moderation

Apply to join the moderator team if you have time. Moderators keep channels clean and welcoming.

## Respecting Others' Work

**Credit when you use it.** If you start from another painter's template or pattern, credit them.

**Ask before modifying.** If you want to remix another painter's livery publicly, ask them first. Most will say yes; a few will say no. Respect the answer.

**Don't claim others' work.** Passing off someone else's paint as your own is the fastest way to get banned from the community.

**Call out theft, don't pile on.** If you see theft, report to moderators privately. Don't organize dog-piles on thieves — moderators handle it.

**Trademark content caution.** Don't use real brand logos (car-manufacturer badges, real sponsor logos) without permission. The community tradition is to use fictional-brand artwork unless you have explicit rights.

## How to Ask for Help

**Search first.** Your question has almost certainly been asked. Use the Discord search or forum search before posting.

**If you didn't find an answer, be specific.** Include:
- What you're trying to do.
- What you tried.
- What happened instead.
- SPB version number (from About menu).
- OS (Windows/Mac/Linux).
- Screenshot or video if relevant.

**Ask in the right channel.** Technical bugs → #help. Design feedback → #work-in-progress. Feature requests → #feature-requests.

**Be patient.** Most answers come within an hour during active times, within a day on weekends. Don't @-mention individuals unless you know them.

**Say thanks.** When someone helps, reply with thanks. Builds community goodwill.

## Showcasing Builds

The #showcase channel is for finished work. To get featured:

**Polish the post.** Good screenshots, clear description, notable design choices called out.

**Time your post.** Peak engagement is evenings local to your audience. For North America + Europe mix, aim for 7-9 PM EST.

**Link related work.** If this livery is part of a series (team set, season set), link the others.

**Engage on comments.** When people comment, respond. Thank them. Answer questions. This is how relationships in the community form.

**Submit to showcases.** Weekly community showcases feature the best work of the week. Submission form pinned in #showcase.

**Consider portfolio building.** Collect your showcased work into a portfolio — a Discord message with thumbnails, a public Google Doc, a personal website. Portfolios help you get picked up for paid work.

## League Participation

Leagues are the real-world application of SPB. To join one:

**Find your fit.** Check #leagues for active series. Look at their config to see if the rules match your painting style.

**Read the regulations fully.** Before joining, read the league's regulations page end-to-end. Rules-shock is the most common reason new members leave leagues.

**Start with a template.** Use the league's supplied template liveries for your first paint. You can build from scratch once you've learned the league's look.

**Communicate early.** If you have a concept outside the usual palette, DM the admin before painting. Better to get approval in concept than rejection after hours of work.

**Attend livery review sessions.** Some leagues run weekly livery review streams where admins walk through approvals and rejections. Watching these teaches you what's accepted.

**Pay your dues.** Most leagues have a nominal donation or fee to cover hosting. Pay it on time.

**Contribute to the league community.** Join the league's Discord, not just the main SPB Discord. Show up for practice sessions. Make friends with your co-drivers.

## Conduct

A few simple rules keep the community healthy.

- **Be kind.** Feedback is critical but never cruel.
- **No harassment.** Zero tolerance for bullying, slurs, or targeted personal attacks.
- **No NSFW content.** Keep designs appropriate for all-ages audiences.
- **No piracy.** Don't distribute cracked SPB builds or unauthorized copies of paid assets.
- **No spam.** Don't cross-post the same self-promotion to every channel.
- **No politics.** Unless the server has a #politics opt-in channel, keep political content out of the main community.
- **Follow moderator instructions.** Moderators keep the space good for everyone. Arguing with them in public gets you timed out.

## Community Rhythm

The community has seasonal rhythms. Knowing them helps you engage.

- **January:** new-year goal setting; lots of new painters; great time to teach.
- **Spring:** leagues launch their seasons; showcase activity peaks.
- **Summer:** slower overall; smaller one-off events; good time for personal projects.
- **Fall:** championship-season; livery quality peaks across the community.
- **December:** winter-themed art liveries; holiday team cars.

Showcase your work when your audience is active, rest when it's quiet.

---

## Becoming a Community Contributor

Long-term, the painters who become known in the community all do similar things:

- Post consistent work (weekly, not sporadic).
- Help new painters in #help.
- Contribute tutorials or recipes.
- Run a league or help admin one.
- Build relationships with other painters (not just one-way engagement).
- Keep improving visibly — each livery better than the last.

You don't need to do all of these. Pick the ones that match your time and energy. The community values consistency over intensity.

---

Showing up matters. Keep showing up, keep painting, keep learning. The community is here to help you grow — and someday, to grow because of what you contribute.
