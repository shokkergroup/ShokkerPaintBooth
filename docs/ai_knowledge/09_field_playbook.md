## Playbook: start of every design session (template / wireframe / Wire / Mask layers off, read zones, two pages)
1) spb_status, then spb_get_car_map (this also warms the car's named parts so apply_scheme sees them). 2) Switch the TEMPLATE layers OFF yourself with spb_edit_layer: Wire, Mask, Car Mandatory (group "Turn Off Before Exporting TGA"). They are sometimes on by default to show the panels, and they draw grids, outlines and fake headlights over the preview you are judging. With Mask off, the dead space between panels shows the base paint colour: that is not on the car and iRacing never shows it. 3) Read the zones (spb_get_zones) before editing: zone numbers move when zones are added on top; use the zone's name or id from the result and check the "queued: edit zone N (name)" line names the zone you meant. 4) If two different designs come back on consecutive reads, two SPB pages are open: ask the buyer to close the other tab before changing anything. (Learned 2026-10-01.)

## Playbook: buyer words: "crushed down to X%" / pattern smaller / pattern size means scale, "a touch of colour" means sparkle, "same shade" means sample
"Crushed down to X%" / "make the pattern X% of the size" = the zone's texture SCALE (scale 0.05-5, and spec_patterns[].scale), NOT spec_strength or intensity. Leave spec strength alone unless they say strength. "A touch of colour" in a dark area = sparkles or flecks of that colour, NOT a gradient fade. "Only the yellow in the logo" = select that colour (+ tolerance) on that art's LAYER inside a small box around it. "Same shade as X" = sample X's colour; do not guess a named colour. "Keep the strokes" = recolour only the fill colour with a tight tolerance and leave outline colours unselected. "Starting from the base file" = clean zones: ask the buyer to reset the zones in the app rather than calling undo many steps. (Owner corrections, 2026-10-01.)

## Playbook: recolour only part of a piece of art (number fill, logo colour)
Use a zone with region {layers:["Numbers"], colors:["#262626"], tolerance: 10-16} so only the fill colour is taken and the white and black strokes stay. Sample the real fill colour first (dark fills are often #202020-#2a2a2a, close to a black stroke, so keep the tolerance tight). Then zoom the preview to confirm the outline and drop-stroke survived. For a colour inside one logo (e.g. the yellow of a spray-can graphic), combine colors + a small rect box around that logo + layers limited to the decal layers. WITHOUT the layer limit the box also grabs body paint whose ORIGINAL colour matches (a pink body zone sitting on a yellow base layer gets turned back to yellow inside the box). Check region_check.takes_pixels_from: it should name only the decal/numbers zone.

## Playbook: gradients run across the whole sheet
A zone gradient is laid over the entire 2048x2048 canvas, not along each part. The car is unwrapped, so a fade on one island does not continue on the bumper or the other side: it will not line up and the buyer notices. Use gradients only for deliberate whole-sheet effects or one-zone-per-panel fades. For "a little colour coming into the black" use a flake/sparkle material instead (see the sparkle card). (Owner: the fade "doesn't line up anyway", 2026-10-01.)

## Playbook: a touch of colour in black: coloured sparkle / flake in a dark area (blue in the black)
What works: a monolithic flake finish as the zone material, then the zone sliders. Black with blue sparkle = monolithic::cf_deep_space (black + deep blue/purple/silver flake) with brightness -45, hue -20 (pushes purple to blue), saturation +60 (makes the flecks read blue, not grey). Measured: about 55% pure black with blue flecks. What does NOT work: paint patterns cannot take a colour (metal_flake draws silver; stardust at high opacity floods the area blue, at low opacity becomes an even navy tint); a second_base over a monolithic special did not show at all. A spec pattern can be added back on top for texture (e.g. spec_patterns snake_scale_diamond, scale 0.25, channels MRC) without changing the colour.

## Playbook: adjust a finish's colour with the H/S/B sliders
Most "brings its own palette" finishes can be steered with hue / saturation / brightness on the zone instead of hunting for another finish. Too dark or navy -> brightness +30 to +40. Leaning violet -> hue -15 (towards sky blue). Grey reading tinted -> saturation -10. Flecks gone grey after darkening -> saturation up. Example that worked: monolithic::fmo_moonstone_adular (blue-white glow over silver schiller) with brightness +35, hue -15, saturation -10 = light ice-blue and steel-grey plates, a real two-colour design with no gradient. (2026-10-01.)

## Playbook: spec-only looks on a solid colour
A monolithic special given a SOLID colour keeps that colour flat and contributes its spec: monolithic::fm_python_skin with color #ff2d95 gave a flat pink with a diamond scale pattern in the spec only. Use this for "keep the colour, give it a <texture> look" on body colours. For art that must stay pixel-identical (numbers, sponsors) use a Foundation finish with color "source" (base::f_soft_gloss for plain gloss). When stacking a chrome fill over gloss decals, keep the special fill zone ABOVE the decal gloss zone (lower index wins) and check spb_get_zones order afterwards.

## Playbook: checking effects the preview cannot show
The preview image is about 768 px for a 2048 px sheet, so fine effects (sparkle, micro flake, holographic grating at small scale, snakeskin at scale 0.25) average out and can look flat or tinted. Do not conclude "it did nothing" from the picture: zoom a crop and measure pixel values, and read spb_preview spec=true. Spec map colours: red = metal, green = roughness, blue = clearcoat (high = dull). Example: base::efx_holographic_drift at scale 0.25 on white accents read flat green in the spec preview but measured metal ~75 vs ~26 for plain gloss, so it was working. Candy, chrome and metal twists are invisible in the flat colour preview: always confirm them on the spec map.

## Playbook: things that went wrong (avoid)
- spb_undo with many steps after the buyer reset the zones by hand restored an OLD state from earlier sessions (dozens of zones). Prefer muting a zone or editing it back; ask the buyer to reset when they want the base file.
- The lightning paint pattern on matte black reads as grey cracked glass, not bolts, at any scale.
- apply_scheme's base colour is limited to the paintable mask: once Mask is hidden, the dead space keeps the old colour. Harmless in iRacing; to cover it anyway use region {everything:true, layers:[body paint layers]}.
- The zone list does not report scale or spec strength: if the buyer set a value by hand, ask for it instead of overwriting it.
- On the ARCA Chevrolet SS the spoiler has no body-paint pixels, so schemes skip it.

## Playbook: make artwork "reveal" / light up when light hits it (face, mask, logo art)
Spec-only, two zones on the art, both color "source" so the picture never changes: (1) the whole art layer -> base::f_soft_matte (shadows absorb light); (2) ABOVE it, the art's light tones only (region layers:[art layer], colors:["#d0d0d0","#a0a0a0"], tolerance ~40) -> base::f_satin_chrome (broad soft flare). In the sim the light parts flash silver as the sun or track lights sweep across, and the dark parts stay dead, so the shape appears out of the paint. Check spb_preview spec=true: chrome (red) should sit only on highlights, matte (teal) on shadows. Watch for other art on the same layer: light text there gets the chrome too (on a RAM truck the MyersMask layer also held a whole hood panel). Use f_chrome for a harder mirror flash. (Owner request "highlight dynamically as light hits it, like a reveal", 2026-10-02.)
