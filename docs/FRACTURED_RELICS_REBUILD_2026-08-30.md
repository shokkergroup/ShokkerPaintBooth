## 1. DIAGNOSIS — why the old 100 feel same-y (and why M7 never caught it)

The five shelves are **combinatorial grids**, not collections of individual objects:

| 🔮 FRACTURED OCCULT | 20 | the only non-grid shelf — and the one the owner wants to build the whole category around |

M7 medians are 86.9–91.1, i.e. the metric says "fine." The metric measures *per-finish* structure,
never **inter-finish sameness of concept** — a recolor of a good finish scores as well as the
original. The owner's eye reads the grid instantly. **A rebuild that keeps the grid fails no matter
what the scores say.**

Secondary defect: the two shelves the owner named (cathedral stained glass, clockwork guilloche)
are *decorative-pattern* families — wallpaper, not artifacts.

## 2. THE NEW ARC — "the cabinet of cursed things"

**FRACTURED RELICS = surfaces from objects that should not exist.** Every finish is a *thing a
collector dug up, stole, or was warned about* — not a material and not a pattern. The category
law, binding on all 50:

1. **It is an OBJECT, not a motif.** Each id is a nameable artifact or remain. If the name could be
   written as `<color> <pattern>`, it is rejected on sight.
2. **Aged ground, always.** Every surface carries history: burial tooth, patina, wax, soot, salt
   bloom, tannin, verdigris. Nothing is factory-new. (The `_age` ply is mandatory.)
3. **Ritual/organic INTENT in the geometry.** Marks were made by someone or grown by something —
   wards, tallies, sutures, growth rings, vessel branching. Never abstract decoration.
4. **The curse wakes in the light.** FRACTURED family DNA: dark cold ground, thin-film hue window
   flooding the ritual lines with iridescent life at angle. This is what makes it a *Fractured*
   category and it is unchanged from its siblings.
5. **8–32px dense pave** (owner's universal law, and the module's proven car-band arithmetic:
   pitch ~10.4 `_S`-units, `_lowcut` knee at r=64). No poster, no one giant sigil.
6. **No confetti, no mangled garbage** (owner, 2026-08-30 — learned on X LAB): every feature is
   *attached* to a structure. Scatter fields must be packed and organized, never free-floating.

## 4. THE SPEC UPGRADE — material states that follow the artifact

Owner: *"the most advanced spec map properties you can possibly build. The spec maps for the most
part should follow the pattern designs. They should ALL have millions of details."*

Base stays the FRACTURED **ghost-shift contract** (M = color amplifier, R = angular aperture,
Cc = power supply carved by macro domains) so the family reads as one. On top of it, RELICS adds a
**structure-locked material-state carve** — the X LAB Hologram-Metal lesson, applied with relic
logic instead of cell logic:

1. **Material plates.** The finish's OWN generator is re-run cheaply (448²) and quantized into 4
   material zones that follow the artifact's anatomy — `void / body / relief / inlay`. Each zone
   owns a discrete (M, R, Cc) centre from the recipe (e.g. *incised void* = dead matte,
   *polished relief* = hands have rubbed it bright, *metal inlay* = chrome, *crust* = rough dull).
   Because the map is the generator itself, **the spec follows the pattern exactly**.
2. **Tool-mark shoulders.** |∇S| ridges get a bright low-roughness shoulder — the worn edge of
   every carved mark.
3. **Three micro plies** (the "millions of details"): structural tooth (`S − blur S`) into R,
   pigment/patina grain (art luma residual) into M, and a domed pit lattice into R/Cc — each
   **gated by material**, so crust pits and metal doesn't.
4. **Metal fleck** — sparse deterministic bright flecks inside inlay zones only.
5. **Burial gradient** — one slow field pushes whole regions toward buried-dead (M down, R up,
   Cc dull). Decorrelates the channels and tells the relic story.

Everything is computed once at WORK=1152 and cached; nothing iterates at render res (perf law).

### The measurement lesson (worth keeping)

Three metrics had to be corrected before they measured anything real:

1. **Absolute fineness measures brightness.** `paint_fn` rescales the art by `val`, so a darker
   finish scores lower no matter how much detail it carries. Gate on **fine ÷ mean luma**;
   calibrated against owner-approved siblings (`_relics_work/calibrate.py`), the family runs
   0.16–0.28.
2. **Mean-of-RGB is not luma.** Using it, hue jumps between features counted as "fineness" —
   inflating exactly the finishes with scattered anchors. Use 0.299/0.587/0.114.
3. **"Spec mirrors paint" cannot be measured against paint luma.** The thin-film LUT is an
   oscillator: a smooth ramp in the structure becomes several luma bands, so the paint's edge map
   is full of colour-band edges the geometry never had, and a spec that tracks the geometry
   perfectly reads as uncorrelated. Measure the spec against the **finish's own generator** —
   which is what the owner's words actually mean. (The catalog gate's own `trace` check carries the
   same caveat in its source: it is advisory because it over-flags owner-approved finishes.)
