First, what this thing is

Shokker Paint Booth makes iRacing spec-map liveries. Every car in iRacing reads a paint file (the colors) and a spec map (the instructions for how light behaves on the surface — what's chrome, what's matte, what's candy, what catches fire when you roll past a corner worker). SPB builds both, automatically, from the finishes you choose.

Three honest things, so we start on the same page:

  • It's a spec-map tool, not a painting app. You're not airbrushing flames pixel by pixel — you're telling light what to do. Think material designer, not Photoshop.
  • There's no spinning 3D car preview. We can't render iRacing's cars and we won't fake it. You design here, render, and look at it in iRacing, where it counts.
  • Your car has no "shininess" slider in real life either. Light just does stuff to chrome that it doesn't do to suede. The spec map is how you boss the light around. That's the whole trick.

The one idea that makes everything click: the spec map

iRacing lights your car using three channels. You never hand-edit them — you pick a finish and SPB writes all three. But knowing they exist explains why a finish looks the way it does.

M · RED CHANNEL

Metallic

Is this surface metal or not? Cranks the difference between a flake-loaded chrome and a flat painted panel.

R · GREEN CHANNEL

Roughness

Mirror-smooth to dead matte and everything between. Low roughness = that wet, glassy look. High = it eats light.

CC · BLUE CHANNEL

Clearcoat

The glossy coat on top. It's where the magic lives — carve a pattern into the clearcoat and the car flashes by angle.

When two finishes look different on the same color, the spec map is the reason. That's it. That's the secret the whole app is built around.

In Depth

Everything above, with the lid off.

The workspace

  • Header / top bar: your iRacing ID + folder, plus Finish Viewer, Spec Sculpt, Shokk Drop, Import Recipe, and Commands (Settings, Spec Channels, About, Shortcuts…).
  • Center canvas: your car, live. When a spec map is loaded you also get a Spec Channels dock to inspect M/R/CC separately.
  • Zone Manager (left): the list of zones you've made.
  • Zone Popout (right): the per-zone editor — Base, Pattern, Spec Patterns, and the HSB sliders. This is your home.

There are pixel-level editing tools on the canvas for touch-ups too. They work, but they aren't the point, and they're still maturing in beta — the power is in the finish-and-spec system.

Setting up iRacing (the part everyone fumbles once)

  • User ID — your iRacing Customer ID (helmet icon in iRacing → Profile): the number iRacing puts in custom-paint filenames. It is not your car number; iRacing only loads files that carry your own ID.
  • Car folder — the folder for that specific car's paints, e.g. …\iRacing\paint\porsche911cup\. Not a file. If you paste a path ending in car_12345.tga, SPB will try to sort it out, but give it the folder and we'll both be happier.
  • Custom Number / Sim-Stamped Number — decides whether SPB writes car_num_<id>.tga (your paint carries its own number; iRacing loads it only with Settings → Graphics → Hide Car Numbers ON) or car_<id>.tga (iRacing stamps your number on; Hide Car Numbers OFF). They must match, or iRacing quietly shows your paint-shop colours. Not sure? Render once in each mode.

SPB writes two files per render: the paint and the spec map (car_spec_<id>.tga). Both go in that folder.

PSD vs TGA — how much control you get

IMPORT A PSD — the power move

Layers map to zones

Bring in a layered Photoshop template and each layer ties to its own zone. That means a finish you put on the body won't bleed onto your sponsor layer, your numbers stay your numbers, and a spec effect on one piece leaves the others alone. Maximum control, clean separation, no cross-contamination.

JUST A TGA — still totally fine

Zone by color

No PSD? No problem. Load your flat TGA and carve zones by color instead. You can do everything — you just have a little less separation between elements, since it's one baked image rather than tidy layers.

Zones — telling SPB where

A zone is a region of the car defined by color. Point at a color, SPB grabs everything that matches, and now you can dress that region however you like.

  • Tolerance — how fuzzy the match is. Low = that exact shade. High = the whole neighborhood. Fixes 90% of "it grabbed the wrong stuff."
  • Hard Edge — crisp boundaries instead of soft blends.
  • Multi-color zones — stack several source colors into one zone when they should wear the same finish.

Patterns — texture and shape

Click the Pattern swatch to lay a pattern over a zone (Abstract/Fractal, Tech/Carbon, Geometry/Deco, Nature, Cultural, the Decades kits, Reactive accents, and more). Scale, rotate, offset it; fit it to the zone or fill the canvas. A note: patterns are built to sit on the car in any orientation, because every car lays its template out differently. Treat a pattern as a material, not an upright decal.

Spec Patterns — the secret sauce

This is what makes an SPB livery an SPB livery. The Spec Patterns section layers detail directly onto the M, R, and CC channels — independently, up to five stacked. It's how a finish stays calm head-on and then ignites when light hits it at the right angle: sparkle, optical interference, brushed-metal direction, carbon weave, clearcoat coatings, structural geometry. You don't need the physics. You need to know the spec layer is why a great livery looks alive in the corners and a flat one looks like a sticker. Spend time here.

Render & see it in iRacing

  • RENDER builds the final paint + spec TGAs and drops them in your car folder (set the folder once and every render lands there).
  • In iRacing press Alt+Tab → Ctrl+R (Reload Car Textures): tweak → render → reload → tweak again. (Auto-Deploy in Settings only matters when no car folder is set.)
  • Not showing up? Open the chat and say it does not show up in iRacing: the built-in helper checks your ID, number setting, folder and files and tells you which step is off.

Rendering should be quick. If a particular finish takes its sweet time, that's worth reporting — we hold ourselves to a "fast, or it's a bug" standard.

More tools worth knowing

  • Spec Sculpt Lab — a full-page workshop for building a spec map from a paint image on its own, when you want to dial light behavior precisely outside the zone flow.
  • Shokk Drop — import your own artwork or a DNA spec, and share finishes as .spbdrop packs.
  • Import / Export Recipe (.shokkerrecipe) — save or restore an entire livery setup in one file.
  • Finish Viewer — window-shop the whole library as thumbnails.
  • Spec Channels — pop open the raw M/R/CC channels of your current spec map to see exactly what SPB wrote.

Settings, license & updates

  • License / Activation — paste your key, hit Activate. There's a Deactivate if you're moving machines.
  • Auto-Update — a banner appears up top when a new build is out; it downloads, then offers Install & Restart. During beta, expect these often. That's the point.
  • Report a Problem — builds a copy-paste diagnostics bundle (recent errors, logs, what the app was doing). It never includes your license key or passwords. When something's weird, this is the single most useful thing you can send us.

Gotchas, in one place

  • The iRacing field wants the folder, not the file.
  • PSD = clean layer-to-zone separation; TGA = works great, just less separation.
  • Tolerance fixes most zone-selection headaches.
  • The spec layer is what separates a flat livery from a living one.
  • For FRACTURED: assign as Base, pick a color, crush the brightness, daytime track.
  • If a render is slow or a finish looks off — Report a Problem. Seriously.
