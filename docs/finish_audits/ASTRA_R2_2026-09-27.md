# ASTRA R2 — all 50 finishes rebuilt (Claude, 2026-09-27)

Owner, on the Codex R1 rebuild: *"surprisingly AWFUL ... give me something to be proud of ... finishes that do what they say,
are UNIQUE, JUMP OFF THE SCREEN, and are the most intricate and detailed you can possibly come up with."*

## What was wrong with R1
All 50 R1 finishes used one grammar (`rebuild_tools.Surface`): sparse cv2-stamped glyph marks scattered on a flat dull ground, six flat
material roles, ~15 lines of recipe per finish. The shelf read as one finish in 50 recolours; low coverage; no depth; legacy M7 41.8-76.7.

## What R2 is
* **`kit.py`** - fast 2048 field primitives (labelled-distance Voronoi, warped fbm, constant-width iso-lines, Turing labyrinth,
  FFT field convolution, SDF figure fields via id-stamped polygons, clothoids, thin-film/cosine palettes, relief), all
  allocation-light (Windows page-faults every fresh 16 MB array - in-place ops are ~4x faster).
* **Five lane modules** (`originals`, `color_shoxx`, `surfs_up`, `mad_scientist`, `future_shoxx`), one hand-authored construction
  per finish, each built from the actual maths of its name. **`r2.py`** holds names, published copy, identity contracts and swatches;
  the 50 stable per-finish modules `adopt()` their construction (ids unchanged; `common.bind` keeps one authored seed and ONE
  native build shared by paint and spec).
* Lane arcs: ORIGINALS = precious matter under starlight; COLOR SHOXX = two pigment populations (matte-dielectric day carrier vs
  dull-coat metal night carrier) with hidden regional lean; SURFS UP = one real sea phenomenon per card; MAD SCIENTIST = specimens
  under the microscope in continuous colour; FUTURE SHOXX = precision-engineered metamaterials.
* **Spec micro-zone layer** (`kit.enrich`): an independent ~20 px clearcoat mosaic + 1 px chrome/matte micro-flakes on every card
  (the Hologram Metal mechanism) - richer specs (effective materials median 32 vs Brass Night 4.6), channel independence for M6,
  FOLLOW untouched (clearcoat is 7% of spec luma, flakes are below the 8-32 px band). Velvet pile and wax body are masked to stay matte.

## Gates (all on the final state)
* **Finish Law** `--group ASTRA --fresh`: **0/50 failures**, 50/50 distinct material stories (ratio 1.00).
* **Uniqueness Law**: **50/50 pass**, nearest neighbour anywhere in the catalog max 46% (fail line 80%).
* **M7** (house formulas, isolated run): **86.0-99.7, 50/50 >= 85** (R1: 41.8-76.7).
* **Real export** (`build_multi_zone` -> 2048 TGA): exactly one native build per finish; exported paint = authored paint
  (MAE <= 0.51/255). Builder time, machine-speed-normalised (a fixed 2048^2 numpy benchmark is run beside every timing):
  **median 1.776 s, max 2.302 s** (+ ~0.6-0.8 s engine stage). The final measurement ran while the machine was
  1.6x slower than idle (CapCut/Creative Cloud), so raw stage times read up to 4.2 s; the quiet-machine full run before the
  2026-09-29 pass measured finish stage 1.43-2.66 s, 0 over 3 s, and the untouched finishes are unchanged.
* Tests: `tests/test_astra_finishes.py` + rewritten `tests/test_astra_rebuild_material_intent.py` - 8/8.

## 2026-09-29 owner-feedback pass ("use this to tweak the lesser performing ones")
Owner's verdicts on R2: **Chromatic Undertow** the standout, **Switchblade Chevron** "VERY impressive", **Bipolar Cyclone** "holds its own",
Spectrum Guillotine effect great but design less loved, Duality Scales "much better at night, doesn't blow me away". Measured why
(`_astra_claude_work/tools/why_undertow*.py`, `why_rank.py`): the winners are *neighbour colour contrast* (true complementary pair at ~9 px) with a
spec that is the **colour-opposite of the paint** - warm paint on the matte population (spec cyan/green), cool paint on the metal population
(spec red/pink) - not raw saturation.
Ten weak finishes were redesigned with that rule (all keep their ids/names): **Abyssal Lanterns** (amber/magenta/coral jellies, polished indigo water),
**Fermion Foundry** (furnace-oxblood ground, momentum-coloured tracks), **Temporal Braille** (backlit blue-to-red panel, complement pins),
**Xenobot Orchard** (lime/cyan/pink bots on oxblood soil), **Negative Space Engine** (blue-violet anodised plate, copper glow), **Sea Glass Confessional**
(saturated backlit regional colour families), **Boardwalk Pinlines** (painted beach-hut planks with complement coachlines), **Meteorite Royal**
(gold / royal-blue / violet lamellae, each its own material), **Tidal Lacework** (orange sand vs cobalt water, thick white lace),
**Causal Origami** (magenta vs teal fold columns - first attempt used Chevron's gold/blue and was rejected on sight as a sibling idea).
Left alone on purpose: Duality Scales and Spectrum Guillotine (the owner likes what they do).

New lesson: **a two-population spec breaks FOLLOW** wherever the populations change but paint brightness does not (FOLLOW 0.02-0.27 on five finishes).
Fix = `kit.r_from_luma`: keep M and Cc free to carry the spec *hue*, solve R from a paint-luma target so spec luma is monotone in paint luma
(FOLLOW 0.76-0.99). The same change lowered channel independence, so Cc is a fully independent 20 px mosaic (`enrich(cc_mix=1)`).

## Lessons worth keeping
* **FOLLOW = roughness must track paint brightness.** Spec luma is 72% roughness; when R is non-monotone with paint luma inside a
  feature (Prism: -0.05) or the ground sits between two feature types (Polarity Lace), FOLLOW collapses. Diagnostic:
  `_astra_claude_work/tools/fdiag.py`.
* **Sub-4 px periodic texture aliases at the law's 1024 measure** and becomes uncorrelated noise in one channel - keep periodic detail >= 8 px.
* **Far-apart hues interleaved finer than the eye averages to grey** at car distance (Quasicrystal, Bismuth, Chromosome...). Fix =
  regional hue drift (each area leans one family) + dark grounds with bright features, never a global rainbow.
* **Rotating coordinates by a spatially varying angle around the origin shears the canvas** (Duality Scales, Reef v1) - warp, don't rotate.
* **Per-pixel int64 hashes and HxWx3 broadcasts are the hidden cost**: hash the label range once and gather; compose colour in place.
