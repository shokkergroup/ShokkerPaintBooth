# SPB Before / After — 10 Transformation Case Studies

> Each case study takes a weak or "default" livery and walks through the exact decisions that turned it into something worth submitting. Read these as an apprentice reads a master's case notes.

---

## Case 01 — Factory Silver → "Liquid Mercury"

**Before:** Stock silver metallic body, black plastic trim, no graphics, no personality. The "rental-car livery."

**After design choices:**
- Swap base to `pearl_base` silver with higher metallic amplitude
- Add `chameleon_base` subtle cool shift (silver → pale lavender)
- Replace plastic trim with `chrome_wrap` and `carbon_weave` rocker
- A single horizontal character line gets a 3mm gloss-black accent

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Replaced entire body base (gloss_metallic → pearl_base) — 4 minutes.
2. Layered chameleon shift at 28% intensity — 8 minutes.
3. Rocker mask → carbon_weave at 70% pattern strength — 5 minutes.
4. Mirror caps + window surround → chrome_wrap — 3 minutes.
5. Hand-drew 3mm character-line accent with mask tool — 10 minutes.

**Time invested:** 30 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- A finish change carries more visual weight than a color change.
- Silver → silver with subtle shift is a bigger jump than silver → red with no shift.
- One well-placed gloss accent line reads "designed," not "decorated."

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't keep the black plastic — it flattens the upgrade.
- Don't over-tune the chameleon shift; above 40% it looks like a prism.
- Don't add graphics — the surface is the graphic.

## Case 02 — Clown-Car Graphics → "Focused Identity"

**Before:** Eight decals, five sponsor logos, three stripe colors, number on every panel. A busy yard-sale.

**After design choices:**
- Delete 60% of decals
- Pick ONE primary sponsor and scale it up 2.4×
- Consolidate stripe palette to two colors max
- Move number to one large door roundel (driver's side only)

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Cleared decals one by one; kept primary sponsor, timing stripe, and number — 12 minutes.
2. Scaled sponsor logo 2.4× and relocated to hood center — 6 minutes.
3. Audited stripe colors, dropped magenta, kept red + white — 8 minutes.
4. Enlarged door number from 18" to 34" — 4 minutes.
5. Re-balanced: added one small tertiary mark on rear quarter for back-of-field identity — 5 minutes.

**Time invested:** 35 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- Real race liveries have 5-8 graphic elements, not 20.
- Scale is cheaper than quantity — one big logo beats three small ones.
- Drivers should see their own number from 30 feet, not their sponsor from 3.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't distribute decals "evenly" — liveries are asymmetric on purpose.
- Don't add a color because it's "a team color" unless the sponsor pays for it.
- Don't keep sponsors that don't pay (in simulation, that means none — fictional sponsors should advance the design).

## Case 03 — Flat Matte Black → "Carbon Phantom"

**Before:** Entire car flat-matte black. Visually a blob. Zero readability in photos.

**After design choices:**
- Keep black, change finishes
- Body → `gloss_wrap` black
- Hood + roof → `matte_wrap` black
- Rockers + mirror caps → `carbon_weave` / `carbon_composite`
- Thin centerline accent in `candy_apple`

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Body finish swap matte→gloss — 3 minutes.
2. Hood + roof kept matte but with `matte_wrap` spec floor raised to 165 — 4 minutes.
3. Rocker carbon_weave, mirror caps carbon_composite — 6 minutes.
4. 6mm candy_apple centerline — 8 minutes.
5. Re-verified spec contrast in light and shadow renders — 4 minutes.

**Time invested:** 25 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- SPB's moat is finish differentiation. One color, three finishes, and the car looks expensive.
- Matte-everywhere becomes a silhouette. Gloss/matte contrast gives the body shape.
- A single colored line across a blackout is the entire livery.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't add brighter colors to "rescue" a blackout — commit.
- Don't matte the lower rockers — they're already in shadow; lose that detail.
- Don't chrome on a blackout — too big a visual leap. Stay in the texture conversation.

## Case 04 — Day-Glow Yellow → "Hornet That Earns It"

**Before:** Full safety-yellow body, no other design. Looks like a rental.

**After design choices:**
- Keep 60% yellow, add `matte_wrap` black hood + roof
- Chevron accent in `candy_apple` red
- Strategic small black numbers (not huge) to offset the yellow
- `carbon_weave` detail on hood scoop

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Added hood + roof black wrap with a forward-swept leading edge — 11 minutes.
2. Red chevron accent running front fender into rear quarter — 14 minutes.
3. Shrank door numbers from 36" to 24" (yellow is already loud) — 3 minutes.
4. Hood scoop detail → carbon_weave — 5 minutes.
5. Added 3mm black pinstripe separating yellow from black zones — 7 minutes.

**Time invested:** 40 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- High-vis colors need relief zones; eye needs rest.
- Pinstripes aren't decoration — they're visual sealants between adjacent colors.
- Less number size when the color is already screaming.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't pair yellow with another loud color (orange, lime). Use neutrals.
- Don't use more than one chrome or candy accent on a yellow body; it crosses into toy territory.
- Don't keep flat uniform yellow; a subtle pearl_base yellow holds light better.

## Case 05 — Off-Brand Patchwork → "Retro Privateer"

**Before:** Mismatched fonts, fake sponsor logos that don't share a design era, random racing stripes.

**After design choices:**
- Pick ONE era (chose early 70s) and commit
- Rebuild stripes in period-correct widths (twin 4.2" / 4.5")
- Hand-drawn imperfect edges to imply tape-masked
- Single period typeface for all text

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Nuked all existing decals, kept only body base — 3 minutes.
2. Re-painted body in off-white pearl_base (warmer than pure white) — 6 minutes.
3. Laid twin stripes with 0.5° intentional off-axis tilt — 15 minutes.
4. Chose one period typeface and used it for all text — 4 minutes.
5. Added 2-inch hand-lettered sponsor logo in one place only — 10 minutes.

**Time invested:** 38 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- Era-consistency is more important than color-consistency.
- Period-correct imperfection beats modern precision for retro builds.
- The font is 20% of the era vibe — get it right.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't mix eras (70s stripe + 90s chrome trim + 20s lettering).
- Don't make stripes too straight — period stripes were hand-masked.
- Don't use an over-saturated red; period reds were duller.

## Case 06 — Everybody-Else's-Blue → "Indigo Owner"

**Before:** Generic racing blue. Looks like 40% of the other cars in the field.

**After design choices:**
- Shift hue from cobalt (#0047AB) toward indigo (#1B1B3A)
- Add pearl_base undertone so blue shifts violet in light
- Add gold-tinted chrome instead of silver chrome

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Hue shift base color 12° toward purple — 2 minutes.
2. Layered pearl_base pearl undertone at 35% — 6 minutes.
3. Swapped all trim to chrome_wrap with warm gold tint — 8 minutes.
4. Darkened upper body 8% so indigo reads in direct sun — 4 minutes.
5. Added a 1mm gold thread along character lines — 12 minutes.

**Time invested:** 32 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- 12° of hue shift separates you from every other "blue car."
- Pearl undertones do the heavy lifting — people don't consciously notice, they just think the car is "fancier."
- Gold + indigo is rarer than silver + blue. Rarity = memorability.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't go purple — indigo is blue-that-thinks-violet, not a violet car.
- Don't add navy secondary color; indigo wants gold or cream, not more blue.
- Don't keep silver chrome; it fights the warmth of indigo.

## Case 07 — "Busy Pattern Everywhere" → "Single Statement Panel"

**Before:** Carbon weave on body, camo on hood, pinstripes on rockers, checkerboard on roof. Every panel is a different pattern.

**After design choices:**
- Pick ONE pattern statement
- Chose roof-panel plaid only
- Body returns to clean pearl_base
- One thin accent pinstripe to echo pattern colors

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Stripped all patterns except roof — 6 minutes.
2. Repainted body pearl_base champagne — 4 minutes.
3. Cleaned roof plaid to period-correct colors (cream/navy/red) — 10 minutes.
4. Added 2mm navy pinstripe along belt line — 8 minutes.
5. Verified spec across all zones for consistent sheen — 3 minutes.

**Time invested:** 31 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- Pattern is like spice — one or two in the whole dish.
- Patterns compete with each other; give one pattern the win.
- A statement panel with clean body elevates the pattern.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't patternize every panel "because you can."
- Don't mix organic patterns (weaves) with geometric (checkers) on the same car.
- Don't use high-contrast patterns on curved panels where warping distorts them.

## Case 08 — Neon Rainbow → "Disciplined Synthwave"

**Before:** Six neon colors (pink, cyan, lime, yellow, orange, purple) all fighting for attention.

**After design choices:**
- Reduce to three colors: deep purple base, hot pink accent, cyan secondary
- Keep the dark field; let the neons pop
- Add a single horizon gradient that unifies the three

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Repainted body deep purple gloss_wrap — 5 minutes.
2. Deleted all color zones except pink + cyan — 7 minutes.
3. Built horizon gradient across lower half (cyan at bottom, pink at horizon line) — 14 minutes.
4. Added subtle 3mm pink pinstripe at horizon line — 6 minutes.
5. Removed all chrome (fights the neons) — 2 minutes.

**Time invested:** 34 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- Neons need a dark field to avoid looking like a crayon box.
- Three colors is the neon max — above that, it's chaos.
- Gradient is a design element, not just an effect.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't use white as a primary with neons — makes it look medical.
- Don't chrome anything; chrome wants cool/warm neutrals.
- Don't use a saturated background; background should be *darker* than accents.

## Case 09 — Pristine But Lifeless → "Lived-In Rally Car"

**Before:** Rally-styled livery but with showroom-fresh paint. Feels fake.

**After design choices:**
- Add subtle `damage_wear_v2` overlay at 15-20% strength on lower panels
- Darken front-fascia with "bug-splatter" texture at 8%
- Leave upper panels mostly clean to show contrast

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Applied lower-body damage overlay at 18% — 5 minutes.
2. Masked front-facing surfaces (grille, hood leading edge) and added bug-splatter at 8% — 9 minutes.
3. Very subtle dust overlay on door handles (drivers touch them) — 4 minutes.
4. Slightly desaturated the body base 4% — the world dulls paint — 3 minutes.
5. Added one intentional deep scratch on front fender (stage-kiss signature) — 6 minutes.

**Time invested:** 27 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- Wear implies use, and use implies a backstory.
- Placement of wear matters more than amount — wear where a human would actually cause it.
- Subtle is the whole game — 20% looks lived-in, 60% looks abandoned.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't apply damage evenly across the body — real wear is localized.
- Don't add rust to a car that isn't supposed to be old — rust is a calendar.
- Don't damage the roof unless there's a reason (rollover, tree branches).

## Case 10 — Afterthought Rims → "Rims As Anchor"

**Before:** Default black rims on a carefully designed body. Rims were the last thing picked.

**After design choices:**
- Treat rims as a featured design element, not a footnote
- Pick rim color from the MOST rare color in the body palette (not most common)
- Match rim finish to one body finish (if body has chrome trim, chrome rims)
- Or deliberately contrast (matte body, polished rims)

**Step-by-step what changed:**
1. Analyzed body palette — identified the accent (not primary) color as the chosen rim color — 3 minutes.
2. Swapped rim finish to `chrome_wrap` with candy tint in the accent hue — 6 minutes.
3. Added a thin matte stripe across the rim to repeat a body graphic — 10 minutes.
4. Verified rim reads cleanly from 15 feet away — 2 minutes.
5. Tweaked caliper color to match rim accent — 3 minutes.

**Time invested:** 24 minutes

**Key lessons learned:**
- Rims are the 5th panel of the car — they're always in view.
- Rims + calipers + lug nuts are a subsystem — treat them as one.
- A tiny accent color on wheels is more visible than the same accent on a door.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't default to black rims on everything; it erases 15% of your design.
- Don't put a loud rim on a quiet body unless contrast is the design thesis.
- Don't forget calipers — they're the earrings to the rim's outfit.

## Transformation Patterns (cross-cutting lessons)

1. **Remove first, add second.** Almost every before→after starts with deletion.
2. **Trade color variety for finish variety.** Three finishes in one color > four colors in one finish.
3. **Rarity wins.** The one-of-one design beats the better-executed common one.
4. **Placement > quantity.** One decal in the right spot beats five in average spots.
5. **Tell a story with wear, texture, and era — not just color.**

If you learn to see your own work as a "before" in a hypothetical "after," you've graduated.
